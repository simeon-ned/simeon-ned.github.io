1 · Where we areThe tracker is mature

1Whole-body control is already here

One unified controller walks, runs, dances, fights, jumps and gets up — tracking practically any reference, on real hardware.

BeyondMimic, SONIC, ZEST, OmniXtreme, GMT[10, 12, 22, 29] differ in details; the recipe has converged — one policy, one motion library, randomized simulation. They are published, open-sourced and already integrated into unified stacks such as wbc-mjlab[15]: the same policy runs on the robot (video) and in a browser tab (live).

The tracker is no longer the scarce component. The question is what to build on top of it.

2 · So what?The open questions

2We have a WBC. So what?

Run in the GPU simulators that trained it (MuJoCo Warp, Newton, Isaac Lab, mjlab[25, 30]), the tracker produces unlimited physical experience: contact-rich, human-like rollouts with every hidden variable observable.

Q1 · CONTRACT

Is a dense reference the right interface?

Today the WBC wants a full-body pose and base motion at 50 Hz. Should a VLA emit joint trajectories — or only sparse intent (key-bodies, a path, a style), with something below filling in the body?

Q2 · BEHAVIOUR

Task-driven, yet natural and robust

“Hand there, walk at this speed” — while looking human, surviving a push, getting up alone. Kinematic generators write motion for an idealised world; a tracker following them blindly is neither robust nor agile.

Q3 · EXPERIENCE — THE MAIN ONE

What to do with the physical experience

Forces, contacts, payloads, terrain, and the failures mocap never shows — at petabyte scale. How do we turn that into generators, estimators and controllers that stay human-like?

Thesis

The WBC and the simulator are an engine of realizable human-like motion with its latent state. Generation, estimation and control are its consumers — tied together by one manifold of human motion learned with flow matching on that output, not on mocap clones.

3 · InstrumentsOne engine, one thread, one stack

3The instruments

I · THE ENGINE

Simulator + frozen WBC as the source of physical experience

Every rollout carries what a robot never observes — forces, torques, payload, friction, terrain — plus the failures mocap never shows. Controller-in-the-loop, with the tracker's own randomisation and sensor noise.[25, 30]

II · THE THREAD

A manifold of human motion learned with flow matching

One model in three roles: generator (autoregressive / latent flow), prior (a cost for RL, IK and sampling planners), conditioning for estimators and the VLA.[11, 7, 20, 14] Fitted to the engine's rollouts — the motions the robot can realize, not only the ones a human performed.

III · LATENT SUPERVISION

Streaming estimators and dense representations

Privileged simulator variables become targets for onboard estimators; dense human→robot correspondences[27] let the engine ingest human data without a per-robot retarget.

4 · DirectionsD1 · Generative Motion Layer

4.1Generative Motion Layer: a reactive generator as the interface to a frozen WBC

A compact latent flow-matching model maps sparse intent (velocity, style, waypoints, key-bodies) and a short proprioceptive history to the next WBC reference, at the tracker rate, with the tracker frozen — an S0.5 layer in the Helix notation.

  • Trained on the engine. Labels come from the tracker's own rollout buffer; untrackable segments are rejected in simulation. The generator learns what the WBC can actually execute.
  • One interface for nominal motion, recovery and loco-manipulation — recovery is generated, not switched by an automaton.
  • Fixed WBC contract. Joystick, teleop, VLA chunks or clips change upstream without retraining the tracker.
Status · proof of concept, September 2026

~12M parameters · trained on BONES-SEED and LAFAN subsets[2, 8] · walk / run / sprint / crouch, soft and hard disturbance recovery · runs with the WBC on the G1 ARM CPU at 50 Hz and in the browser.

Next: loco-manipulation and key-body commands under disturbance; an action-chunking generator family; a depth / height-map channel. Write-up: Motion Layer proposal.

4 · DirectionsD2 · Latent estimators · with Anton Chaplygin

4.2Latent estimators: the engine gives every hidden variable for free

Simulated rollouts carry labels a robot never sees. Streaming estimators trained on the same buffer turn them into onboard signals that feed the WBC, the Motion Layer and the VLA.

A · SIGNALS

Contact · wrench · velocity

Contact confidence, external wrench and base-velocity residuals from proprio history, conditioned on the same motion-manifold latent as the generator.

B · TERRAIN FROM VISION

Height maps as a latent

Train a terrain-aware WBC with a privileged height map; an estimator on the same rollouts recovers it from the depth channel. Tracker and Motion Layer consume the estimate — no separate mapping stack.

C · PARAMETERS · LATER

Plant parameters from interaction

The same rollouts label payload, friction and actuator state; physically consistent estimates from proprioception once loco-manipulation with objects is on the robot.

Estimation is how the upper layers become aware of the plant — a payload change or a slippery floor becomes a conditioning input, not a surprise.

4 · DirectionsD3 · Modalities and reactiveness of the VLA · with Innopolis students

4.3Additional modalities and reactiveness of the VLA

Today's VLAs emit action chunks open-loop at 5–10 Hz; the body is left to a controller that was never told what the plan assumed.

Sparse intent in, trackable body out

The VLA emits key-body targets, paths and style; the Motion Layer fills in a state-aware body at 50 Hz. Full joint trajectories from a foundation model are neither necessary nor robust — WholeBodyWAM and LYRIC converge on the same boundary.[33, 32]

More modalities, faster loop

Condition the fast visuomotor layer on proprioception, estimated contacts / wrench / latents (D2) and the generator's tracking state; adapt chunk length to uncertainty; guide a frozen flow policy with success / failure traces.[5, 38] The motion manifold is its action prior.

The engine as the VLA's data factory

Physically consistent, labelled full-body trajectories — contacts, recoveries, style switches, loco-manipulation — instead of kinematic retargets.

5 · RoadmapSep 2026 → May 2027

5Roadmap

NOW · Sep–Oct 2026

Working proof of concept

  • Motion Layer + frozen WBC on the G1 CPU at 50 Hz; walk / run / disturbances on hardware.
  • Loco-manipulation and key-body commands under disturbance; action-chunking generator family.
  • Streaming estimators on the same buffer: contact, wrench, base velocity.
2–3 MONTHS · to Dec 2026

A working interface

  • Natural whole-body motion from joystick, waypoints, loco-manipulation targets or VLA tokens.
  • Transfer to the target robot; generative full-body teleop from sparse points, no kinematic retarget.
  • Terrain-aware WBC trained with a privileged height map.
  • First integration with a high-level VLA.
3–8 MONTHS · to May 2027

Vision and expansion

  • Depth cameras in the Motion Layer — terrain-aware locomotion with vision: stairs, obstacles.
  • Estimators on the terrain-aware WBC recover height maps from the visual channel.
  • Autonomous data pipelines in simulation; a sim set the VLA can train on.
LONGER

Ordinary behaviour

  • A terrain-aware loco-manipulation contract between any intent source and the tracker, sim and hardware.
  • A simulation factory: privileged goals label contacts, recoveries and style changes mocap never covers.
  • Grow the Motion Layer toward S1 (vision, language tokens) or distil it into S0.
6 · What I proposeTeam, partners, pipeline

6What I propose

1 · TEAM

A small team, hired fast and precisely

Strong people from MIPT, Innopolis and Yandex who already ship RL / control code on hardware. Small enough to keep the WIP limit; senior enough that each owns a direction end-to-end.

2 · LAB

A lab at Innopolis University, the company as its sole customer

Create or partner a lab at Innopolis; I am responsible on the company side. The company sets the problems and owns the results; the lab supplies students, compute and an academic home. Wider academic collaboration is welcomed by the rector, Alexander Gasnikov.

3 · PARTNERS

Informal collaboration with a lab at KAIST

Co-authors of our tracking work are at KAIST: joint publications, shared benchmarks, exchange on tracking and retargeting — no contractual overhead.

4 · PIPELINE

Teaching as the talent funnel

I am a Senior Lecturer at Innopolis and already teach there. Dedicated Robotics Control courses on exactly what the stack needs — RL for legged systems, whole-body control, generative motion, simulation tooling — and the best students go to internships in the relevant teams.

First-year outcome for the company

A reactive generative interface between its high-level models and its WBC, running on the robot's CPU · estimators that make plant and terrain visible from proprioception and cameras · a simulation data factory feeding its VLA · a team and a university pipeline that keep building on the same engine.
